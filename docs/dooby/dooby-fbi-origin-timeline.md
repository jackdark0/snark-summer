## H1 — download vs. screenshot: the story changes per case
The single most-repeated dodge. Whether he *downloaded* CSAM or merely *screenshotted* it flips depending on which sounds less incriminating, and he resists "saved a copy" until cornered.

- **Case A — the PDF (downloaded).** VID2 ~2:46: *"i've had cases where people sent me like a pdf for example full of c-sam and images of torture... in that case yes i downloaded c-sim directly to my computer because it was in the fucking pdf that i was sent... i did hold on to it and i reported it yes."* → flat admission he downloaded and retained CSAM. [clip](https://youtu.be/DDvOvz1IAoQ?t=166)
- **Case B — the 12-year-old's account (screenshotted).** VID2 ~5:55: *"Did you download that? Unfortunately, i didn't download it. i screenshotted it."* Then immediately, when North says "you saved it on your computer": *"Yeah, if you want to say i saved a copy of it, sure."* [clip](https://youtu.be/DDvOvz1IAoQ?t=355)
- **the hole:** "downloaded" and "screenshotted to my computer" are the same act (CSAM saved to his device); he treats them as morally different and only concedes "saved a copy" under pressure. the distinction tracks rhetorical convenience, not a real difference.

## external verification (checked 2026-06-23 against primary sources)

**Headline: the central mechanism in H2 is backwards / unsupported.** The two specific, named services he leans on do the *opposite* of what he describes, and the "FBI analyst hands a civilian a secure portal to upload CSAM directly" relationship isn't a thing. The villains (764) are real; the parts where *he personally* gets credit don't check out.

1. **The reporting pipeline (H2) — CONTRADICTED.**
   - **Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org)** hashes the image *on your device*; "your image or video will remain on your device and will not be submitted." Only the **hash** goes to NCMEC and participating platforms for takedown. It does **not** upload images and does **not** put you in touch with the FBI. Dooby repeatedly describes *uploading images* to it — backwards. (And North correctly told him it's "not meant to get you in contact with the FBI.")
   - **NCMEC CyberTipline** is an online report form (report.cybertip.org); reports route NCMEC → law enforcement. The standard architecture is: platforms auto-detect via PhotoDNA hashing → auto-report → NCMEC forwards → investigators build the case, *then* make contact. There is no step where a civilian serial-tipster gets a personal "FBI analyst" who DMs them a secure upload portal.
   - **The FBI does not provide civilians a secure portal to upload CSAM.** Public reporting is the CyberTipline or tips.fbi.gov / 1-800-CALL-FBI — generic tip intake, not a CSAM upload channel.
   - **RAINN** ("the rain website") is the national anti-sexual-violence org / National Sexual Assault **Hotline** — not a reporting conduit that links you to the FBI. He's repurposed a hotline org into a "step-by-step links to report to the FBI" portal it isn't.
   - **Bigger problem for the whole story:** guidance is to **report the location** of CSAM, not to collect, save, and forward the material yourself. "I had to download/save CSAM to preserve evidence and send it to the FBI" runs against how reporting actually works — saving CSAM is the crime, not the remedy.
   - sources: [Take It Down FAQ](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/faq/), [Take It Down how-it-works](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/takeitdown-how-it-works-handout_updated-5.3.23.pdf), [CyberTipline report form](https://report.cybertip.org/), [FBI tips](https://tips.fbi.gov/), [RAINN](https://rainn.org/)

2. **764 / the threat landscape — TRUE (he's not inventing the villains).** 764 is a real, designated, transnational sextortion/accelerationist network that coerces minors into producing CSAM and self-harm; real UK arrests of members exist (e.g. Cameron "Acid" Finnigan, Horsham, sentenced 6 years Jan 2025). So the backdrop is legitimate — which is exactly what makes the personal-credit claims easy to smuggle in.
   - sources: [764 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/764_(organization)), [ISD: Networks of Harm](https://www.isdglobal.org/publication/networks-of-harm-a-victim-centric-information-resource-on-the-764-sextortion-network-2/), [GNET: designation of 764](https://gnet-research.org/2026/03/16/the-designation-of-764-network-why-does-it-matter/)

3. **UK arrests for threats against Meghan Markle (VID1 ~2:34:36) — UNVERIFIED, likely embellishment.** Real pieces, no connecting thread: there *were* real "disgusting and very real" far-right threats against Meghan Markle in the UK with prosecutions (per Neil Basu, ex-head of UK counterterror), and there *are* real UK arrests of online extremists. But **nothing links a group Dooby personally infiltrated/reported to UK arrests for Markle threats** — and the Markle threats were far-right, a different milieu from 764's satanic/nihilistic accelerationism. Classic pattern: true backdrop, unverifiable "and that was me" claim. [clip](https://www.youtube.com/live/8uIQffyoB1k?t=9276)
   - source: [CBS: threats against Meghan Markle](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/meghan-markle-duchess-sussex-disgusting-very-real-threats-uk-far-right-neil-basu/)

4. **CCleaner as the "FBI-directed" CSAM wipe (VID2 ~17:50) — WEAK / implausible as stated.** CCleaner is a real consumer tool with a secure-delete/overwrite mode, so it *can* be used for anti-forensic wiping — but (a) there's no FBI recommendation of it, (b) forensics firms routinely recover evidence *despite* CCleaner use, and (c) the FBI coaching a civilian on how to securely destroy CSAM (which is contraband/evidence until handed over) is not a thing they'd do. The tool is real; the "an FBI analyst told me to purge it with CCleaner" framing is unsupported. [clip](https://youtu.be/DDvOvz1IAoQ?t=1070)
   - source: [Magnet Forensics on CCleaner](https://www.magnetforensics.com/resources/oh-no-the-suspect-ran-ccleaner-to-get-rid-of-the-evidence/)

5. **The "105-year sentence" perp (VID3 ~1:26:05) — checkable in principle, unverified.** Dooby claims a specific case he helped catch: a man who got *"105 years of prison time... commuted to ~10... then caught from prison messaging love letters [to] a 13 year old... another 25 years."* That's a distinctive sentencing fingerprint that should be findable in court/news records **if a name surfaces** — currently no name, so unconfirmed. Same embellishment risk as the Markle claim: vivid specifics, no handle to verify. [clip](https://youtu.be/MNpW_WTX-9s?t=5165)
